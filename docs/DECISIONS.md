# Decisions

One line each: decision, then why.

- **2026-09-28 · TypeScript 5.9 (not 7.x).** Stable, well-known compiler API; TS 7 is newly released. Revisit later.
- **2026-09-28 · Node 24 LTS installed via winget.** Nothing was installed; LTS is the safe default.
- **2026-09-28 · Crouch is bound to C only, not Ctrl.** Ctrl+W closes the tab and can't be blocked outside fullscreen.
- **2026-09-28 · Crouch lowers eyes (and later the hitbox) but not the movement capsule.** No crawl spaces in Phase 1; avoids stand-up-inside-geometry bugs.
- **2026-09-28 · Characters don't physically collide with each other in Rapier.** The sim will handle spacing and BB hits; keeps physics simple and deterministic.
- **2026-09-28 · Gravity 20 m/s², jump apex 0.68 m (measured), 0.55 s cooldown, 30 m/s terminal fall speed.** Snappier than real gravity; jump is a hop, not a traversal tool.
- **2026-09-28 · Walkable ledges ≤ 0.15 m.** Rapier autostep with a capsule only clears ~0.15 m reliably (measured); maps avoid ledges between that and jump height.
- **2026-09-28 · FOV is configured as 100° horizontal at 16:9, Hor+.** Three.js FOV is vertical; a fixed vertical FOV avoids fisheye on ultrawide while matching CS/Valorant-like width on 16:9.
- **2026-09-28 · Default sensitivity 0.001 rad per mouse count.** Close to the CS default (0.00096), a familiar baseline; slider 0.2×–4×, stored in localStorage.
- **2026-09-28 · Commands are keyed by character id (Map), not array index.** Bots joining/leaving can't misroute input.
- **2026-09-28 · Level materials are MeshLambert with merged geometry per texture.** Cheap on integrated GPUs; a few draw calls for the whole map.
- **2026-09-28 · Pixel ratio capped at 1.5, PCF shadows (2048 map).** High-DPI laptops with iGPUs can't afford native resolution at 60 FPS; PCFSoft was removed in three r186.
- **2026-09-28 · Rapier compat build (inlined WASM) in its own chunk: 4.3 MB raw / 1.7 MB gzip.** Simplest loading; well under the 30 MB budget. Three.js is also split out (0.5 MB / 0.13 MB gzip).
- **2026-09-28 · Dev-only `?nolock` flag.** Automated browsers can't take pointer lock; needed for verification.
- **2026-09-28 · Features add only the code they use.** Critic review of the scaffold (attempt 1) flagged speculative code; command fields, bindings and helpers now arrive with their features.
- **2026-09-28 · All level blocks collide as closed 12-triangle trimeshes with FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES, not cuboids.** Rapier 0.21's capsule controller sinks into any cuboid (any size) along its x = ±z diagonal planes; convex hulls and round cuboids too. Without FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES, face-diagonal edges give ghost ground contacts (wall sticking, crate climbing).
- **2026-09-28 · Kill-height safety net (`MapData.killY`).** Any character below it returns to its spawn with no momentum; defence in depth against physics glitches.
- **2026-09-28 · Ceiling detection uses a tolerance (`ceilingBlockFraction` 0.5).** Rapier trims upward moves by ~1e-5 m while sliding along walls; a strict check killed jumps next to cover.
- **2026-09-28 · Game keys only suppress browser defaults while playing.** Paused menus (slider arrows, Space on buttons) keep native behaviour.
- **2026-09-28 · Sun shadow frustum is fitted to the level's box in light space.** Guarantees every corner of the map gets shadows.
- **2026-09-28 · Per-chunk size budgets via a tiny build plugin (Rapier 4.5 MB, others 800 kB).** Vite has only one global limit, which Rapier's inlined WASM forces high.
- **2026-09-28 · Standing characters move horizontally only, then a ground probe places them 0.04 m above the floor; Rapier snap-to-ground is off.** Gravity in the main move made Rapier reject whole moves (~1 stall/s); snap-to-ground bumped 3.5 cm at triangle seams; resting at exactly the 0.02 m skin let moves graze the floor and lose up to 24% speed.
- **2026-09-28 · First map "Depot" is mirror-symmetric across x = 0 and roofless.** Mirror symmetry is the simplest fair layout for 3v3 elimination (each team's north lane is the same lane); a roof adds nothing to Phase 1 gameplay and would complicate lighting.
- **2026-09-28 · Cover heights: full ≥ 2.4 m, crouch cover exactly 1.2 m (crates, barriers, window sills).** Crouched eyes (1.09 m above the floor) hide behind it; standing eyes (1.66 m) see and shoot over it — a readable peek/duck rhythm. 1.0 m cover did not hide a crouched player.
- **2026-09-28 · Map layout rules are unit-tested: symmetry, cover heights (unclimbable, crouch cover hides), clear spawns, three connected lanes, no sightline between anyone within 5 m of either spawn, no straight lane line over 23 m, no sightline over 30 m between points on a 1 m grid (finer scans still find a few; see KNOWN_ISSUES).** Keeps fights at AEG/CQB range and maps honest as they change.
- **2026-09-28 · Only the ground probe decides "grounded" (it needs an upward-facing surface under a sphere narrower than the capsule).** Rapier's controller reports ground when the capsule's rounded bottom touches the top edge of cover, which let players hang on and hop over 1 m barriers.
- **2026-09-28 · Crouched hitbox top must stay ≤ 1.15 m above the floor (hitboxes arrive with the hit feature).** Crouch cover is 1.2 m; a taller crouched head would poke over cover to a standing shooter.
- **2026-09-28 · Prop tints come from a hash of each block's mirror-invariant position, from palettes without team blue/orange.** Mirror twins always match and prop colours never suggest a team side.
- **2026-09-28 · BBs fly at 88 m/s (AEG) and 72 m/s (pistol), slower than real replicas (~100 m/s).** Keeps BBs visible in flight (~0.45 s across 30 m) without making them easy to dodge.
- **2026-09-28 · Hop-up = Magnus lift ∝ speed × exponentially decaying spin; drag is quadratic.** Gives the airsoft arc: AEG within ~5 cm to 20 m and ~20 cm at 30 m, then clearly dropping; the pistol drops ~18 cm by 20 m. Tuning lives in config/ballistics.ts and config/replicas.ts; the shape is unit-tested.
- **2026-09-28 · An empty trigger pull clicks dry and starts a reload automatically.** Fun beats simulation; a manual R reload is still there.
- **2026-09-28 · Recoil is a small sim-side aim kick (0.18° AEG, 0.5° pistol per shot, recovers in ~0.1 s) and the camera shows it.** Replicas are toys: light kick, but the crosshair must stay honest about where BBs go.
- **2026-09-28 · Replica models: recognisable real-world silhouettes (AR-pattern AEG, polymer striker pistol), stylised rendering, two-tone (black + tan). Orange tips optional, off by default (VIEWMODEL.orangeTips).** User direction: guns should resemble real ones without AAA realism; toy feel comes from sound, recoil and BBs. Still no brand names, logos or copies of a specific trademarked design.
- **2026-09-28 · First-person hands with gloves and a team-colour armband on each sleeve.** Airsoft sites mark teams with tape; seeing your own team colour on your arm is readable and on-theme. Team colours live in config/teams.ts and are kept off neutral props (hue-distance test).
- **2026-09-28 · All sound is synthesised (Web Audio), no audio files.** Mechanical, plasticky replica sounds; BB impacts are positional and rate-limited.
- **2026-09-30 · Reloads tilt the replica about its own grip while the magazine (a separate part) slides out and back in; hands stay put until Phase 2 reload animations.** Keeps the replica on screen and off the crosshair, and hints at a mag change cheaply; the magazine itself is mostly below the frame until Phase 2 animations bring the support hand in.
- **2026-09-30 · BBs and impact puffs have a minimum on-screen size (angular radius in BB_VISUALS / IMPACT_PUFFS).** Readability beats true scale (pillar 4); 6 mm BBs and small puffs vanish past ~10 m.
- **2026-09-30 · Friendly hits count (HITS.friendlyFire), as at real sites.** Authentic and encourages care; a config switch if it proves frustrating with bot teammates.
- **2026-09-30 · Hit volume = body capsule (radius 0.2 m, boots to shoulders) + head sphere (radius 0.13 m, top 1.75 m standing / 1.13 m crouched); arms and replica don't count.** Matches the drawn figure (the figure is built from the same numbers, tested), so hits look fair; 1.2 m crouch cover fully hides a crouched player.
- **2026-09-30 · Hit players call for 1.4 s (hand up, "HIT!" sign), then walk straight to their team's dead zone; after 4 s anyone still walking is moved there.** There's no navigation until bots; the walk-off will use their waypoints later.
- **2026-09-30 · A minimal round loop ships with hits: a team with nobody in play ends the round and everyone respawns 4 s later, with referee whistles. Once the round is decided it's a cease-fire: nobody can fire and BBs still in flight hit no one.** Needed to play hits more than once; the cease-fire mirrors the end whistle and stops post-round friendly fire. Score, timer and first-to-5 come with the rounds feature.
- **2026-09-30 · Teams read by a team-colour chest rig and armbands on greybox figures; everyone wears goggles.** Readability at range beats realism (pillar 4); eye protection is part of airsoft's identity.
- **2026-09-30 · While out you spectate over the shoulder of someone still in play (teammates first, click to cycle).** Matches "can still spectate"; keeps the eliminated player engaged.
- **2026-09-30 · Navigation is a 0.2 m walkability grid built automatically from map blocks (A* + string pulling), not recast-navigation.** Depot is flat and box-built, so a grid is exact, has no dependency and needs no authoring; route searches are rationed (1 per tick) and take ~2 ms worst case.
- **2026-09-30 · Walk-offs follow nav routes to the dead zone; a walk-off that can't finish (stuck or over 14 s) makes the player "leave the field" (fade out in place) and reappear in the dead zone.** Replaces the straight-line walk and its teleport workarounds.
- **2026-09-30 · Bots are ordinary characters driven by PlayerCommands from ai/ (outside the sim), with human limits: 120° view, line-of-sight checks 10×/s, 0.3–0.55 s reaction, 4.5 rad/s turning, aim error 4.5° settling to 1.1° over 0.9 s, bursts, leading targets by half the BB flight.** Keeps bots on the same rules as the player and makes them beatable; all tuning lives in config/bots.ts.
- **2026-09-30 · Bots advance along hand-placed lane routes (map data), fight, take cover when under fire or reloading, and search last-known/heard positions; they never fire with a teammate near the line of fire.** Simple, readable behaviour on a CQB map; friendly fire counts, so bots must be careful.
- **2026-09-30 · Once a bot has swept its lane it hunts: it heads for whichever of a few random walkable spots lies in the map sector its team visited least recently (4 m sectors).** Guarantees hiding players are found (no round timer yet) without authored search routes.
- **2026-09-30 · Matches are first to 5 round wins; rounds last 2:30 and a round that runs out of time is a draw (nobody scores).** No objectives yet, so time-outs can't favour a side; 2:30 is ample for a 44 m CQB map (bot rounds take ~10–40 s).
- **2026-09-30 · The match result reuses the start screen ("You win!" / "You lose", the score from your side, rounds played and drawn, "Play again"), shown once the match-over whistles have finished (~4.2 s after the deciding hit, derived from the audio config).** No new menus (Phase 1 scope); the delay lets the final HIT! and whistles play out. In-game banners are worded from your side too ("Your team wins the round", "You lose the match"), and the scoreboard marks your team.
- **2026-09-30 · Phase 2 controls: Shift = walk (slow, quiet), Left Alt = sprint, with a key-bindings settings screen.** Owner's choice, matching CS/Valorant walking; Ctrl was rejected because Ctrl+W closes the browser tab and can't be blocked outside fullscreen.
- **2026-09-30 · Phase 2 stays on one map (Depot, enlarged and opened up); the woodland map moves to Phase 3.** Owner's call: improve the core game on a single map first.
- **2026-09-30 · Releases stay alpha on the v0.1 line until at least Phase 3 is done: Phase 2 = Alpha 2 (`v0.1-alpha.2`), Phase 3 = Alpha 3, Phase 4 (polish) possibly Alpha 4.** Owner's call (git tags can't contain spaces, hence the dots).
- **2026-09-30 · No multiplayer, ever planned: the old Phase 4 (multiplayer) is removed and Phase 5 (polish) is now Phase 4.** Owner isn't interested in multiplayer; the deterministic, command-driven sim stays for testability.
- **2026-09-30 · Key bindings are saved in the browser; a key belongs to one action and taking a used key swaps the two.** Nothing is silently left unbound; Esc and OS keys can't be bound, and binding Ctrl shows a warning (Ctrl+W closes the tab).
- **2026-09-30 · Depot grows from 44 × 28 m to 50 × 32 m with the same layout spread out; crate sizes unchanged.** Owner found it cluttered and small: lanes, doors and gaps widen, cover density drops ~25%; sightline caps scale with it (lane 26 m, any 34 m).
- **2026-09-30 · Saved key bindings that would leave any action keyless are discarded as a whole (back to defaults); debug keys (` F3 ]) are reserved and can't be rebound or taken.** Can only happen with corrupt or outdated saves; a clean reset is simpler and safer than a partial repair, and reserving debug keys means no hidden key ever does something unlisted.
- **2026-09-30 · Footsteps: running (heard by bots within 11 m) and sprinting (16 m) make noise, landing a jump thuds (12 m); walking and moving crouched are silent.** The CS/Valorant rule players already know, and it gives the walk key a clear purpose; eliminated players walking off make no bot-audible noise so they never fake a contact.
- **2026-09-30 · Arm hits: undecided, left to the owner's playtest (M3).** Today only the body capsule and head count (arms and replica don't). Test whether a BB clipping a visible arm and not counting feels unfair; if so, add forearm capsules to the hit volume.
- **2026-10-01 · Versioning: the number is the product, the suffix the development state (policy in CLAUDE.md §7).** Everything up to the first feature-complete game is v0.1: alpha builds (`v0.1-alpha.N`, letters for interim checkpoints), then `v0.1-beta` builds once the owner calls it feature complete, then `v0.1` as the first stable public release. Then `v0.1.x` for fixes and small changes, and `v0.2`+ only for a substantially expanded product. Owner's call. It replaces the 2026-09-30 "one alpha per phase" mapping: alpha build numbers mark meaningful releases rather than phases, and Phase 4 may be alpha or beta. The existing builds keep their names.
- **2026-10-01 · Tags are always dotted: the checkpoint's tag is `v0.1-alpha.2a`, and later builds follow the same form (`v0.1-alpha.2b`).** Owner's call. The owner renamed the earlier hyphenated tag to `v0.1-alpha.2a` on 2026-10-01; the owner handles tagging.
- **2026-10-01 · Roadmap regrouped by stage: Phases 2–4 are all alpha. The old "Phase 4: Polish" is split: its new content and systems (art pass, VFX, lighting, menus, full settings, onboarding) stay in Phase 4 as "Presentation and onboarding". Its optimisation, final balance and polish move to an unscheduled Beta section with the other beta-type work.** Owner's call: focus on alpha, beta isn't near. Work is placed by its nature: adding to the game is alpha; finishing it is beta.
- **2026-10-01 · Varied routes: each round a team deals its bots onto lanes by a plan: split (one per lane), pair (two share one) or stack (all on one), weighted 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.2. Lane order is shuffled and each lane point moves up to 1.2 m at random. Bots sharing a lane set off 0.8–1.6 s apart.** Rounds stop playing out the same way, while staying on the hand-made lanes that suit Depot's layout. Stacks make for the occasional rush.
- **2026-10-01 · Moving as a team: a bot more than 7 m ahead of its rearmost bot teammate (towards the enemy side) waits at lane points. The pause at the point counts, so it stands still at most max(pause, 4 s) per point. The player is not waited for. On Blue (two bots plus the player) a "pair" plan splits instead, since a pair of two would be a stack.** Keeps pushes together without a formation system. A player hanging back must never stall the bots, and the cap means a stuck teammate can't either.
- **2026-10-01 · Crouch-peeking: at crouch-high cover a bot ducks, then stands up to look 2–3 times (0.6–1.1 s each). Seeing someone, it fights from the spot without sidestepping and ducks again after 1.2–2.4 s, or at once when shot at, reloading or losing sight. Ducking with the enemy still in sight renews its looks. A target that's out ends it, and the whole cover episode is capped at 12 s. At full-height cover it waits, then moves on as before.** The classic airsoft barricade fight: pop up, shoot, duck. It's readable (you see heads come up) and gives you windows to shoot back.
- **2026-10-01 · Cover search also tries the spot right behind every low block in reach (crates, barriers, window sills: the top between crouched and standing eye height; 0.45 m behind, or 0.8 m if that's blocked), and crouch cover counts as 4 m closer.** Random candidates alone almost never landed in a crate's small shadow: only ~5% of cover was low and peeking hardly happened on Depot. A larger bonus would send bots running past nearby walls under fire.
- **2026-10-01 · On spotting someone at least 8 m away while advancing or searching, a bot first gets behind crouch cover within 3.5 m (if any) and fights from it. Closer contacts are fought on the spot.** How players at a site trade BBs from barricades. It also gives you a beat at range while the bot repositions. Bot-only fuzz (12 matches): low cover ~41% of cover choices, 54 fights from cover (was 1 before this rework).
- **2026-10-01 · Bots walk (silently) for the last 12 m to where they saw or heard someone.** Their own footsteps no longer always give them away when they close in, matching the rule the player plays by.
- **2026-10-01 · Arm hits stay off; the reload hand and pose stay as they are.** Owner playtested v0.1-alpha.2a and had no issues.
- **2026-10-01 · M4 is split in two: M4a (difficulty levels, close-range lethality, per-enemy contacts) and M4b (crouch-peeking, team movement, varied routes, walking).** Each half fits one critic cycle.
- **2026-10-01 · Bot difficulty: Easy / Normal / Hard, chosen on the start screen (saved in the browser), Normal by default. Each level is a set of skill values (BOT_SKILL in config/bots.ts: reaction, turning, aim error, lead, bursts); perception, hearing and tactics are the same at every level.** Skill is what makes bots feel unfair; same senses keep behaviour readable whatever the level.
- **2026-10-01 · The level applies to every bot, teammates included (like CS bot difficulty).** Symmetric teams keep round outcomes about you, not about a mismatch between bot teams. Easy to make per-team later if playtests want it.
- **2026-10-01 · A difficulty change on the pause screen mid-match starts with the next round; on the title or result screen it applies at once.** Never changes a fight in progress.
- **2026-10-01 · Bots open each new contact with a hasty aim: the aim error starts 60–100% of its full size off target in a random direction and settles from there, and its starting size is at least aimErrorStartMetres (Normal 0.75 m) off however close the target is.** Before, a bot's error started at zero, so up close its first BB nearly always hit about 0.5 s after it saw you (~its reaction time): unwinnable for a human. Now the first BBs visibly whizz past and walk onto you.
- **2026-10-01 · Bots aim worse at targets moving across their view (sideways speed × aimErrorTracking metres off).** Slow BBs and moving targets: strafing up close now buys you ~0.4–0.5 s on Normal instead of sweeping you through the bot's BB stream. At 10–16 m it's about even (KNOWN_ISSUES).
- **2026-10-01 · Normal keeps a median ~0.75–0.9 s to hit at 3–5 m (25% of close duels within ~0.7 s).** A player who reacts in time (~0.25 s plus a flick) wins most close duels; the remaining speed is limited by how fast aim settles, which also sets long-range accuracy. Owner playtest decides if it needs more.
- **2026-10-01 · Scratch vectors used only inside a single function call may stay module-level (perception.ts, botCombat.ts); state that lives across calls goes on the Bot.** Avoids per-tick allocations without hidden coupling between steps.
- **2026-10-01 · Difficulty targets, median time from a bot first seeing you to hitting you (15–40 seeded duels, standing / strafing): Easy ~1.7 s / ~1.8 s at 3–6 m; Normal ~0.8–1.25 s / ~1.15–1.5 s at 3–6 m and ~1.9 s at 16 m; Hard ~0.45–0.65 s (about the old bots up close, slightly slower at 3 m).** Tested as an ordering (easy > normal > hard) plus a Normal close-range floor; the exact numbers are for playtesting to confirm.
- **2026-10-01 · Each bot remembers each enemy it has seen separately (last seen, contact start, when its reaction delay ends).** Switching back to someone seen within contactGrace keeps that contact (no new reaction delay); the reaction runs on the clock once started, even through a brief loss of sight.
- **2026-10-01 · Objective mode (M5) is "Attack / Defend" (first called "Flag"; renamed by the owner so Capture the Flag can be its own mode later): each round one team attacks a flagpole in the other team's half and raises its flag by standing within 1.6 m of the pole for 5 s (6 s at first: the pole rarely decided a round); defenders at the pole pull it down (3 s from the top); with both teams at the pole the flag doesn't move, and with nobody there it stays where it is. A raised flag wins the round for the attackers, a time-out for the defenders, and a wipe-out for whoever is left (a double wipe-out is a draw). Overtime: if time runs out while the attackers are working the rope (raising or contesting it), play goes on until they stop, for at most 15 s; the clock shows "OT".** Modelled on flag games at real sites: physical, readable (you see the flag climb) and it rewards holding ground over chasing kills. A half-raised flag staying put makes tagging the pole worth something and forces defenders to come and pull it down. Overtime avoids the most likely "that was unfair" moment, a flag nearly up at the buzzer (critic, M5 attempt 2).
- **2026-10-01 · Attack / Defend keeps the spawns; attack and defence swap at half-time (after round 4 of a first-to-5). The player's team (Blue) attacks first.** Depot is mirror-symmetric, so swapping roles is fair without moving anyone; attacking first teaches the mode by doing. With first to 5, the decider is always in the second half.
- **2026-10-01 · Depot's poles stand in mid at x = ±9.5, z = 0, just east (west) of the crouch barrier. Map tests: mirror images, ≥ 90% standable ground within the rope's reach, reachable by the attackers through all three lanes, attackers' shortest walk > 1.6 × the defenders' longest (measured ~35 m vs ~17 m), and out of sight of the attackers' spawn.** The barrier gives defenders cover over the pole and attackers a spot to crouch at it; the crate yard gives both sides angles.
- **2026-10-01 · The flagpole is visual only: no collider, and BBs pass through it.** A 7 cm pole adds nothing to cover and a collider would change the nav grid and the layout tests.
- **2026-10-01 · The mode is picked on the start screen (Elimination / Attack / Defend, saved in the browser, Elimination by default). Before the first match it applies at once; picked mid-match it starts with the next match.** Like difficulty, never changes a match in progress. The difficulty picker became a general `OptionPicker` used for both.
- **2026-10-01 · Attack / Defend bots: attackers walk their lane only as far as the first point past the middle of the map, then go to the pole, crouch at a spot within 0.8 m of it and stay (fighting anyone they see). Defenders hold the first point of their lane (nearest home), or the second (the first bot on a shared lane, or 30% of the time), never all on one lane; they go after noises within 12 m of the pole but hold their post and watch towards ones further off. Once the flag is off the bottom the two defenders in play nearest the pole run to pull it down (the next nearest takes over if one is hit); the third holds its post. Nobody hunts.** Gives each side a job that reads at a glance (push the pole / hold around it / retake it) using the existing lanes and modes. Bots know how far up the flag is without seeing it: a flag on a pole is in plain view at a real site. Attackers first walked the whole lane, and the critic (M5 attempt 1) found the pole hardly mattered (1 capture in 50 rounds); cutting to the pole at midfield, two retakers instead of all three (so tagging the pole doesn't empty every lane) and a 5 s raise give, in bot-only matches (16 seeds, Normal, after the bug pass), 12 captures in 119 rounds, a flag raised in 7 of 16 matches and attackers winning 50% (a guard test checks these). Unopposed attackers raise it in ~20 s.
- **2026-10-01 · Attack / Defend HUD: ATK/DEF tags on the scoreboard, a strip under it with the flag's height and a line from your side ("Their flag is going up!" pulses as a warning), a marker over the pole with its distance (pinned to the screen edge when out of view, hidden within 5 m), a painted ring on the floor at the rope's reach that takes the colour of whoever works the rope, and a rope ratchet you hear at the pole every 1/12 of the way.** You must always know your job, where the pole is and whether you're winning it.
- **2026-10-01 · Roadmap rewritten from the owner's feature list. v0.1 is the core game with strong foundations: the two existing replicas (AEG, gas pistol), the two existing modes (Elimination, Attack / Defend) and Depot. Phase 3 becomes "Core foundations": magazines and reloads (M7), a BB physics pass (M8), movement and positioning (M9) and a Depot rework to the field checklist (M10). New modes, fields, replica platforms, loadouts, customisation, progression and team communication go to later versions (proposed v0.2–v0.5 in ROADMAP.md).** Owner's call: get the core right before adding content. It replaces the old Phase 3 ("Content": shotgun, DMR, SMG, loadout screen, range, cosmetics, woodland), whose items moved to later versions.
- **2026-10-01 · Limited ammunition means carrying a set of magazines: a reload swaps in the next mag and keeps the old one with what's left in it (no topping up), and there's no refill during a round.** Owner's pick among mag-based ammo, mid/hi-cap mags, resupply and longer reloads; the others can come later with more platforms.
- **2026-10-01 · Team Deathmatch, when it comes, is respawn TDM (hit players walk back and re-enter); Elimination stays as its own mode.** Owner's call: an easy introduction where you're never out for long.
- **2026-10-01 · Loadouts are free from the start; progression, when it comes, is earned by unlocking replicas and gear, never by levels. Team communication (action wheel, pings, hand signals) waits until the bots are good enough to act on it.** Owner's direction.
- **2026-10-01 · Design rules: movement and positioning matter more than raw weapon stats; replicas differ mechanically (loading, cycling, sound, handling), never in damage (one hit is one hit); every field is built to a checklist (cover, barricades, buildings, windows, doorways, choke points, flanking routes, objectives, dead zones / spawn areas).** Owner's direction, recorded in VISION.md and ROADMAP.md.
- **2026-10-01 · Bots hold fire if a teammate is in the line of fire now or will be by the time the BBs get there (the teammate's position moved on by its velocity × the BBs' flight time).** Bug pass before v0.1-alpha.2: a soak of 48 long bot matches found bots hitting teammates who ran into BBs already in the air (13 hits; Attack / Defend makes it likelier as everyone converges on the pole). With the look-ahead it halved (7, mostly on Easy); friendly hits count, as at a real site, so the rare leftover is logged rather than chased.
- **2026-10-01 · Only full releases get git tags (`v0.1-alpha`, `v0.1-alpha.2`, `v0.1-alpha.3` …); no letter checkpoints. The owner removes `v0.1-alpha.2a` and `v0.1-alpha.2b` once `v0.1-alpha.2` is tagged.** Owner's call: a clean, organised tag list. Playtests between releases use plain pushed commits. Replaces the letter-checkpoint part of the 2026-10-01 versioning policy (CLAUDE.md §7 updated).
- **2026-10-01 · Branches: `main` holds the latest stable release. During the v0.1 cycle all work goes to `main` (its tagged commits are the releases); later (about when v0.2 starts, owner decides) work moves to an `alpha` branch, tested builds go to `beta`, and only tested releases are merged into `main` and tagged.** Owner's call: keeps `main` something people can download and play, with room for alpha and beta testing once the game has players.
- **2026-10-01 · Leaning: hold Q / E to lean left / right around cover (owner request after the v0.1-alpha.2 playtest).
